Chess Rules for Beginners

Chess is played by two players on an 8×8 board. White moves first, then the players take turns. The goal is to checkmate the opponent's king.

How the pieces move

  • King — one square in any direction.
  • Queen — any number of squares in a straight line or diagonal.
  • Rook — any number of squares horizontally or vertically.
  • Bishop — any number of squares diagonally.
  • Knight — in an “L”: two squares one way and one square to the side. It can jump over pieces.
  • Pawn — one square forward (two on its first move) and captures one square diagonally forward.

Check and checkmate

A king that is attacked is in check and must get out of check on the next move. If there is no way to escape, it is checkmate and the game is over.

Special moves

  • Castling — the king moves two squares towards a rook, and that rook jumps to the other side of the king. Neither piece may have moved before, the squares between them must be empty, and the king may not be in, pass through or land in check.
  • En passant — when a pawn moves two squares and lands beside an enemy pawn, that pawn may capture it as if it had moved only one square, on the very next move.
  • Promotion — a pawn that reaches the last row becomes a queen, rook, bishop or knight.

How a game can be drawn

Stalemate (the player to move has no legal move but is not in check), agreement, threefold repetition of the same position, 50 moves by each side without a capture or pawn move, or when neither side has enough pieces left to checkmate.
