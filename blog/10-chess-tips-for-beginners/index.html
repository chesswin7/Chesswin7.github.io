10 Chess Tips Every Beginner Should Know

By the ChessWin7 Team · 4 November 2026

You don't need to memorise long opening lines to start winning. These ten habits will win you more games than any trick — and they work at every time control.

1. Control the centre

The four central squares (d4, e4, d5 and e5) are the most important on the board. Pieces in the centre reach more squares, and pawns there give your pieces room. Starting with 1.e4 or 1.d4 is a great habit.

2. Develop your knights and bishops

In the first moves, bring your knights and bishops off the back row. A piece that hasn't moved isn't helping. A good rule: knights out before bishops, and aim them at the centre (Nf3, Nc3).

3. Castle early

Castling puts your king behind a wall of pawns and brings a rook towards the centre. Try to castle within the first 10 moves. A king left in the middle is the most common reason beginners lose quickly.

4. Don't bring your queen out too early

The queen is your strongest piece, so your opponent will chase it with knights and bishops — developing their pieces while you lose time moving the queen again and again.

5. Don't move the same piece twice in the opening

Unless you win material or must escape a threat, every move in the opening should bring a new piece into play.

6. Before every move: checks, captures, threats

Ask three questions before you touch a piece. What checks can my opponent give? What can they capture? What are they threatening? Then ask the same about your own move. This one habit prevents most blunders.

7. Know what the pieces are worth

Queen 9, rook 5, bishop 3, knight 3, pawn 1. Don't trade a rook for a knight unless you get something big back, and count the pieces before every exchange.

8. Watch out for early traps

The square f7 (f2 for White) is protected only by the king at the start. Keep an eye on it in the first moves: a knight on f6 and a pawn on g6 stop most early attacks.

9. Put your rooks on open files

Rooks are strongest on files with no pawns. After castling, connect your rooks (nothing between them) and move one to an open file.

10. Learn the basic checkmates and review your games

Know how to mate with king and queen, and with king and rook, against a lone king. Then look through our 6 checkmate patterns every player should know. After a game, take a minute to find the move where things went wrong — that is how players improve fastest.
