6 Checkmate Patterns Every Chess Player Should Know

By the ChessWin7 Team · 4 November 2026

Most checkmates follow a few patterns. Once you know them, you will spot them for yourself — and see them coming against you.

1. Back-rank mate

How to avoid it: once the opening is over, make some space for your king with a pawn move such as h3 or h6.

2. Scholar’s mate

A beginner’s trap aimed at f7. Defend it with ...g6 or ...Qe7 and the attack fails — and the white queen has wasted time.

3. Smothered mate

Only a knight can do this, because it jumps. Look for it when the enemy king is in the corner with its own rook and pawns around it.

6. Ladder mate

The easiest way to finish a game when you have two heavy pieces. Keep the rooks far from the enemy king so it can’t attack them.
